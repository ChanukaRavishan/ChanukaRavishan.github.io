Research

My work connects trajectory analysis, unsupervised learning, and interpretable machine learning. I study how to identify unusual movement while explaining the behavioral changes behind each detection.

LIAD & Agentic-LIAD

I developed LIAD, a lightweight framework for individual-level mobility anomaly detection. It combines Temporal Signature Matching for weekly behavioral changes with an Origin-Return Encoder for deviations within individual trips. Its explanations describe changes in travel distance, speed, dwell time, destination choice, visited locations, and route structure.

My research includes parallel Python pipelines for more than 14 million trajectory records, evaluation of unsupervised scoring, supervised baselines, and Deep SVDD, and Agentic-LIAD, an extension using large language models to assess evidence and rerank anomalies.
